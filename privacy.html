1. Who we are

Catch of the Day LLC (“Catch of the Day,” “we,” “us”) operates whale-watching tours out of Hoonah, Alaska (Icy Strait).

COTD Captain’s Log is an internal crew logbook for our boat captains. It is not a consumer app for tour guests and is not intended for children.

2. Who this app is for

The app is built for Catch of the Day captains to record tour operations: times, boat, passengers (count, and a daily manifest if the captain imports one), weather and sea state, wildlife sightings, customer vibe and notes, fuel, engine hours, photos, and a GPS trip track (if location is allowed).

This policy covers the captain iOS app. A separate admin app and a company sync server exist in our plans but are not deployed. Those will need their own review, and this policy must be updated before they are turned on.

3. The short version

  • We do not show ads, and we do not use the app to track captains or guests across other companies’ apps or websites.
  • On first launch, the app asks the captain to acknowledge: “Catch of the Day collects your trip location and logbook entries for company auditing. By continuing, you acknowledge this.”
  • In the current shipping app, that information stays on the iPhone. There is no company server in use, and iCloud sync is not turned on. Logs can be opened in the Files app on that same phone, and the captain can share a PDF, CSV, or photo using the iOS share sheet.
  • Location, microphone, speech recognition, camera, and photo library are used only when the captain allows them, and only for the logbook features described below.
  • Spoken notes are turned into text. The app does not keep the audio recording. When the iPhone can transcribe on the device, it does. Otherwise Apple’s speech service may be used.
  • If we later turn on a company sync server or iCloud Drive for auditing and backups, we will update this policy and the app before that data leaves the phone automatically.

4. What the app stores

The following are stored in the app’s on-device database and, when a day is closed out, in a daily archive folder on the phone (PDF, an editable copy, and a data file, plus any tour photos). In the current build, that folder is the app’s Documents folder, visible in the Files app under On My iPhone → Captain’s Log. Nothing is deleted automatically.

4.1 Captain profile and boat setup

  • Captain name (the profile is created on first launch and can be edited in Settings)
  • Boat and engine names, hour-meter readings, and upkeep items
  • FareHarbor account shortname and related settings, if entered
  • A record that the captain acknowledged the auditing notice, and when

API keys for optional services (FareHarbor, or an optional cloud note-reader) are stored in the iPhone Keychain on that device only. They are not written into the log archive and are not in iCloud.

4.2 Tour logbook entries

For each tour, the app may store:

  • Tour name, boat, captain name, start and return times
  • Passenger count
  • Weather and sea state
  • Wildlife sightings (species, count, place, notes)
  • Places along the trip (for example Hoonah Harbor, Icy Strait Point, Point Adolphus)
  • Customer vibe (Positive, Meh, or Bad) and an optional typed or spoken note
  • Free-text notes and incident notes
  • Fuel added or used, optional fuel cost, and engine hours
  • A GPS trip track (see Location, below)
  • Photos the captain adds to the tour

The daily archive and the Print Year PDF include this operational information so the company can keep a printable record. Search on the Log tab and on-device Spotlight can find archived days by captain, vessel, notes, wildlife, places, and customer vibe.

4.3 Location

If the captain allows location access, the app records the trip route and speed only while a tour is running. Tracking starts when the captain starts a tour and stops when the tour is ended. Points are stored with latitude, longitude, time, speed, and course, and are used to fill distance, average speed, top speed, and nearby place names. The log still works if location is denied; that tour simply has no track.

The app may ask for Always location so the track can continue with the phone in a pocket or the screen locked. The system location indicator is shown while a tour is tracked in the background. The captain should stop the tour at the dock.

In the current app, tracks stay on the phone. They are not uploaded to a Catch of the Day server. Usage text in the app states that tracks are also intended for company auditing when that sharing is turned on.

4.4 Photos

The captain may take a photo with the camera or pick one from the library to attach to a tour (for example a whale sighting). Photos are stored with that tour and in the day’s archive folder. They leave the phone only if the captain shares them (for example through the iOS share sheet).

4.5 Microphone and speech

The captain may hold a button and speak a log note or a customer-vibe note instead of typing. The app uses the microphone and Apple’s speech recognition to turn speech into text.

  • The app keeps only the resulting text in the log. It does not write an audio recording to disk.
  • It uses on-device recognition when this iPhone supports it, so it can work on the water without a signal.
  • If on-device recognition is not available, Apple’s speech service may process the audio in order to return text. Catch of the Day does not receive that audio.
  • The log still works if the microphone or speech recognition is turned off; the captain can type instead.

The on-device reader that turns a spoken sentence into fields (wildlife, fuel, and so on) runs on the phone and needs no account. A separate, optional cloud assist parser is off by default (see below).

4.6 Daily manifest (optional)

The captain can show a sample (practice) manifest, import a CSV export from FareHarbor, or — only if FareHarbor keys are saved in Settings — refresh a live manifest. A live or CSV manifest may include guest contact name, party size, tour time, and, if those columns are present, phone number and email.

That information is stored on the phone so the captain can run the day’s trips. Sample data is fake and labeled as sample. FareHarbor keys stay in the Keychain on that phone.

4.7 Optional cloud note parser (off by default)

Settings includes an optional “cloud assist” that can send the text of a log note to a cloud language model to help parse it. This is off until the captain turns it on and saves an API key. If it is turned on, the words of that note leave the phone and are processed by the provider configured in Settings. The on-device parser remains the default and works with no key and no signal.

5. How the data is used

We use this information for Catch of the Day operations and company auditing, including:

  • Completing and reviewing a tour log
  • Building the daily archive, day PDFs, yearly Print Year PDFs, and CSV exports
  • Recording route, distance, and speed for a trip that was actually run
  • Reminders for engine upkeep
  • Averages of fuel and other operational figures
  • Optional review of a guest manifest for that day’s trips

We do not use this information for advertising, and we do not sell it.

6. Sharing and third parties

6.1 What we do not do

  • No advertising networks
  • No analytics or crash-reporting SDKs in the current app
  • No “tracking” in Apple’s App Tracking Transparency sense (we do not link this data with data from other companies’ apps or websites for advertising, or share it with data brokers)
  • No automatic upload to a Catch of the Day server in the current app

6.2 Sharing the captain chooses

The captain may share a day-log PDF, a Print Year PDF, a CSV export, a GPX track, or tour photos using the iOS share sheet (for example to Mail, Files, or Instagram). What happens after that is controlled by the app or service the captain picks. The app can copy a short Instagram caption for the captain to paste; Instagram Stories sharing is available only if a Facebook App ID is configured, which it is not in the current app.

6.3 Apple

If the iPhone cannot transcribe speech on the device, audio may be sent to Apple for speech recognition, only to return text. Apple also provides the iOS platform (including, if we later enable it, iCloud). Apple’s handling of that data is described in Apple’s own privacy policy.

6.4 FareHarbor (optional)

If the captain saves FareHarbor keys and refreshes a live manifest, the app requests that day’s bookings from FareHarbor. FareHarbor is a booking provider the company already uses. This does not happen unless those keys are saved and the captain asks for a refresh.

6.5 Optional cloud language-model provider

Only if cloud assist is turned on. The default settings point at an OpenAI-compatible API; the captain can change the model and base URL. That provider then processes the note text.

6.6 Planned company sync and iCloud (not on today)

The app is built so that, with company approval, a future version could send daily logs, trip tracks, and the auditing acknowledgment to a Catch of the Day server for management review, or store the daily archive in an iCloud Drive folder named Captain’s Log.

Neither is on in the current shipping app. The sync server address is empty, and the current signed build does not include the iCloud entitlement. We will update this policy before turning those features on.

7. Retention

There is no automatic deletion on the phone (and none is planned on a future company server). Logs, tracks, photos, archives, and manifests stay until someone deletes them on purpose. Deleting a tour in the app does not delete a day that has already been archived. If a phone is lost or erased, data that existed only on that phone may be lost, because there is no company backup until sync or iCloud is turned on.

8. Permissions the captain controls

In iOS Settings → Captain’s Log, the captain can allow or deny location (When In Use, and optionally Always), microphone, speech recognition, camera, and photo library. The logbook remains usable if these are denied, with the matching feature turned off (no track, typed notes only, no new photos).

9. Children

This app is an internal workplace tool for adult captains. It is not directed at children and is not intended for use by anyone under 13 (or under 16 where that is the relevant age). We do not knowingly collect personal information from children through this app.

10. Security

Data is stored in the app’s sandbox on the iPhone. Optional API keys use the device Keychain. We do not have a company server receiving this data today. Captains should use a passcode or Face ID on the phone, and should be careful when sharing PDFs or CSVs that may include operational notes or, if a manifest was imported, guest contact details.

11. Changes to this policy

If we change what the app collects, where it is stored, or how it is shared (including turning on company sync or iCloud), we will update this policy and the date above, and we will notify captains in a reasonable way, for example in an app update.
